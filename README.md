# budgeted-repair

[What Memory Cannot Supply: Budgeted Repair and Convention Inheritance in Small Systems](https://standardgalactic.github.io/budgeted-repair/budgeted-repair.pdf)

A set of small experiments on systems that repair themselves or settle on shared conventions under fixed budgets. Every experiment is plain Python with no model downloads and no GPU. Each one bounds its retained state and caps what a single run may write to disk, so a full run leaves tens of kilobytes. Two qualifications apply to those claims. In the repair scripts the beam width bounds the number of search candidates retained between rounds, which makes it the search-retention budget, but a round also generates all successors of the beam before pruning them, and a visited-state set grows until it reaches fifty times the beam width and is then cleared. Total working state therefore scales with the beam width without being equal to it, and no memory measurement is taken. Likewise the byte ceiling applies to the detail and transcript files of one run. Summary files are small and uncapped, and repeated runs into new folders accumulate without limit.

The accompanying essay, `budgeted_repair_essay.tex`, reports the experiments with tables, definitions and a reproducibility section that lists script digests and seed schedules.

## Repair under a search-retention budget

Three scripts damage a known object by a given number of random point edits and ask a beam-search repairer to restore its behaviour. Each repair step is a single edit with cost 1. Success is behavioural: a repaired object may differ in structure from the original, and the scorer only checks agreement with the original on the checks it is given. In `repair.py` the objects are arithmetic expressions and success means agreement on every input in a fixed range. In `repair_graph.py` they are dependency graphs on eight nodes, and success means no cycle and the correct reachability on a random sixty percent of node pairs. In `repair_circuit.py` they are boolean circuits of four inputs and six gates. The repairer sees ten of the sixteen truth-table rows, and the repaired circuit is then compared with the original on all sixteen, which is equivalence of behaviour and not of wiring. Random edits can cancel or change nothing, so the damaged object need not be at edit distance k from the original, and the within-k statistic compares repair cost with the number of applied edits, not with an optimal cost.

In the conditions tried, beam size mattered when the damage was large relative to the beam, and a beam of about 32 reached complete success for expressions and graphs, with larger beams mainly adding states examined. Beam conditions use different damaged instances, so comparisons across beams are unpaired and carry no uncertainty estimate. The circuit script reports three quantities with explicit denominators. The field `visible_ok` is the share of all trials in which a circuit passing the ten visible rows was found. The field `correct_all_trials` is the share of all trials in which the repaired circuit matched the original on all sixteen rows. The field `correct_given_visible` divides the same count only by the trials that passed the visible rows. Visible success is 0.95 to 1.0 in every cell while correctness is between 0.79 and 0.96 under either denominator, and enlarging the beam did not reliably close the gap. The repairer ranks candidates only by visible error with ties kept in generation order and uses no preference for small edits and no model of how the damage arose, so the result is a plateau for that ranking rule. It is consistent with ambiguity in the visible rows but does not show that the ambiguity is irreducible.

## Convention formation under three budgets

`conventions.py` runs a naming game among ten agents holding weighted lexicons over twelve meanings. A speaker names a meaning, a hearer guesses, and a failure is repaired by revealing the intended meaning. The symbol inventory (6, 12 or 24 symbols) sets how much compression the community faces, each agent forgets its weakest entries beyond a cap of 24, and an optional bounded log of recent corrections can be consulted when interpreting a symbol. Every 400 interactions a fraction of the population is replaced by naive newcomers. The symbol inventory dominates the outcome: success is about 46 percent with 6 symbols, 75 percent with 12 and 91 percent with 24, and ambiguity falls correspondingly. At 20 percent turnover the vocabulary survives almost intact. A correction log gave small changes that were inconsistent in direction, with a gain at 12 symbols and no improvement observed at 6.

Retention is defined as the number of meanings whose earlier consensus exists and equals the later consensus, divided by all twelve meanings. A meaning with no entries has no consensus and counts as not retained. Consensus takes each agent's highest-weight symbol per meaning, breaking ties within an agent alphabetically and across agents by agent order.

`turnover.py` raises the replacement fraction to 50, 80 and 100 percent. With no inheritance, about 84 percent of the vocabulary survives an epoch at 50 percent replacement, about a quarter at 80 percent, and about nine percent at full replacement, which is close to the 1/12 expected by chance. Copying a veteran's correction log to newcomers did not help. `seeded_turnover.py` instead seeds each newcomer with the K strongest entries of one veteran, and retention under full replacement rose from about 9 percent at K=0 to 99 percent at K=12. These two scripts differ in several respects at once and compare two particular mechanisms.

`inheritance_comparison.py` gives all rules the same representation and insertion weight under full replacement, but each rule shapes its own donor population over ten cycles, so it measures cumulative effects of a policy and not the immediate effect of selecting content from a given donor. It showed a large advantage for strong entries over recent corrections and random entries.

`matched_inheritance.py` is the controlled version. One reference community is evolved without replacement, snapshots are taken every 400 interactions, and every rule and budget is applied to the same snapshot with the same donor assignment. It also reports pairs actually delivered, distinct meanings covered, the share of meanings carried by at least one newcomer, conflicting symbols per newcomer and agreement with the earlier consensus. A fourth rule, strong entries with ties broken at random, was added after the first run showed that the original strong rule broke ties by meaning index and so gave every newcomer the same meanings, covering only 40 percent of meanings at K=3. The results revised the earlier reading. Most of the large gaps in the cumulative design came from the rules' effects on the donor population. Coverage across newcomers mattered as much as agreement of items. With ties broken at random, strong entries kept a modest advantage over random sampling after 400 interactions, with paired retention differences of about 0.05 and success differences between 0.02 and 0.08. Logged corrections delivered fewer pairs (8.0 of 12 at K=12) covering fewer distinct meanings, with more conflicts, and were worse than random sampling at K=6 and K=12 by 0.21 and 0.41 in retention after 400 interactions, while at K=3 the two were indistinguishable after 400 interactions. Every channel carried substantial convention compared with no inheritance, so no claim is made that only strong items carry a convention. That corrections concentrate on contested symbols remains a hypothesis consistent with the measured conflicts and low delivered counts, and the concentration itself was not measured.

## Running

Each script takes a seed, a trial count and an output folder, and has its own default folder so runs do not overwrite one another. The commands are `python3 repair.py --seed 1 --trials 200`, `python3 repair_graph.py --seed 1 --trials 100`, `python3 repair_circuit.py --seed 1 --trials 100`, `python3 conventions.py --seed 1 --trials 20`, `python3 turnover.py --seed 1 --trials 20`, `python3 seeded_turnover.py --seed 1 --trials 20`, `python3 inheritance_comparison.py --seed 1 --trials 30` and `python3 matched_inheritance.py --seed 1 --trials 30`. They write to `results_expression`, `results_graph`, `results_circuit`, `results_language`, `results_turnover`, `results_seeded`, `results_inheritance` and `results_matched` respectively. The convention scripts import `conventions.py`, `matched_inheritance.py` also imports `inheritance_comparison.py`, and `repair_graph.py` and `repair_circuit.py` import the capped writer from `repair.py`, so the files must stay together. The independent unit in the inheritance comparisons is the run, with the ten cycles inside it averaged and not counted as replicates.

## Limits

Trial counts are small, from 20 to 200 per cell. Only the two inheritance comparisons report standard errors, so differences of a point or two elsewhere should be treated as suggestions. The domains are deliberately toy-sized. The language agents are probability tables with simple reinforcement, and donors in the inheritance scripts come from a community that has already converged, so transmission from noisy or minority donors is untested.
